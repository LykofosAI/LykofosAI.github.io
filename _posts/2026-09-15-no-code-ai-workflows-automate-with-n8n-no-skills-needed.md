You have a few options for running n8n: you can sign up for their cloud service, install it on your desktop, or host it on your own server. For beginners, the n8n Cloud is often the easiest way to start. Once you're set up, you'll be greeted by the workflow canvas – a blank space waiting for your creativity.\n\nThink of n8n as your digital Lego set. Each "brick" is called a **node**, which represents a specific application (like Slack, Google Sheets, or OpenAI) or an action (like sending an email, formatting data, or setting a timer). You connect these nodes with lines to create a flow of information and actions. The interface is intuitive: drag a node onto the canvas, configure its settings in the sidebar, and connect it to the next step. It's a visual language for automation, designed to be understood at a glance.\n\n## Building Your First AI Workflow: A Practical Example\n\nLet's put theory into practice. Imagine you're a content creator, a small business owner, or simply someone who wants to stay updated on a particular topic without manually sifting through countless articles. We'll build a workflow that automatically fetches articles from an RSS feed, uses AI to summarize them, and then posts those summaries to a channel (like Slack or Discord) or saves them to a Google Sheet. This saves you time and ensures you're always informed.\n\n**Step 1: The Trigger – RSS Feed Reader**\nEvery workflow needs a starting point, a trigger. For our example, we'll use an RSS feed. This node will periodically check a specified RSS feed for new articles.\n\n1.  **Add the RSS Feed Reader node**: On your n8n canvas, click the "Add New Node" button (usually a plus icon) or type `RSS` and select "RSS Feed Reader".\n2.  **Configure the RSS Feed**: In the node's settings panel on the right, find the "URL" field. Enter the RSS feed URL of your chosen news source. For instance, you could use `https://techcrunch.com/feed/` for tech news. You can also specify how often it should check for new items (e.g., every 15 minutes). Close the settings panel.\n\n**Step 2: The AI Brain – OpenAI (for Summarization)**\nNow that we have our articles, let's bring in the AI to summarize them. We'll use the OpenAI node, which provides access to powerful language models like GPT.\n\n1.  **Add the OpenAI node**: Connect the RSS Feed Reader node to a new OpenAI node. You'll likely need to select "Completions" or "Chat" as the operation, depending on the current OpenAI node version and model you choose. For summarization, "Chat" with a GPT-3.5 or GPT-4 model is highly effective.\n2.  **Authenticate OpenAI**: If it's your first time, you'll need to add your OpenAI API key. Go to your OpenAI account, generate an API key, and paste it into n8n's credential setup. This securely connects n8n to your OpenAI account.\n3.  **Configure the Prompt**: This is where the magic happens. In the OpenAI node's settings, you'll define the instruction for the AI. Under the "Messages" section, click "Add Message". Set the "Role" to "user". For the "Content", you'll craft your summarization prompt. Here's an effective one:\n    ```text\n    Summarize the following article text in 3 concise bullet points, focusing on the key takeaways and main arguments. Do not include any introductory or concluding phrases.\n    \n    Article Title: {{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["title"] }}\n    Article Content: {{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["content:encoded"] }}\n    ```\n    Notice the `{{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["title"] }}` and `{{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["content:encoded"] }}`. These are n8n's **expressions**, which allow you to dynamically pull data from previous nodes. Here, we're taking the title and full content from the RSS feed node and feeding it to OpenAI.\n\n**Step 3: The Output – Slack/Google Sheets**\nFinally, we need to deliver our AI-generated summary to where it's most useful.\n\n*   **Option A: Post to Slack**\n    1.  **Add the Slack node**: Connect the OpenAI node to a new Slack node.\n    2.  **Authenticate Slack**: You'll need to connect your Slack workspace. Follow the prompts to authorize n8n to post messages.\n    3.  **Configure the Message**: In the Slack node's settings, select the "Channel" where you want the summaries to appear. For the "Text" field, use an expression to combine the original article title with the AI summary:\n        ```text\n        *New Article Summary:*\n        *Title:* {{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["title"] }}\n        *Link:* {{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["link"] }}\n        *Summary:*\n        {{ $node["OpenAI Chat Chatbot"].json["choices"][0]["message"]["content"] }}\n        ```\n        (Note: The exact path to the OpenAI summary might vary slightly based on the model and response structure; typically it's `choices[0].message.content`).\n\n*   **Option B: Save to Google Sheets**\n    1.  **Add the Google Sheets node**: Connect the OpenAI node to a new Google Sheets node.\n    2.  **Authenticate Google Sheets**: Connect your Google account, granting n8n access to your spreadsheets.\n    3.  **Configure the Sheet**: Select the "Operation" as "Append Row". Specify the "Spreadsheet ID" (from the URL of your sheet) and the "Sheet Name". In the "Values" field, map the data from previous nodes to your sheet columns. For example:\n        ```json\n        {\n          "Title": "{{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["title"] }}",\n          "Link": "{{ $node["RSS Feed Read"].json["link"] }}",\n          "Summary": "{{ $node["OpenAI Chat Chatbot"].json["choices"][0]["message"]["content"] }}"\n        }\n        ```\n\n**Test and Activate Your Workflow!**\nOnce your nodes are configured, save your workflow. You can manually test it by clicking the "Execute Workflow" button to see the data flow through each node. When you're satisfied, toggle the workflow "Active" button (usually in the top right corner) to let it run automatically!\n\n## Beyond the Basics: Expanding Your AI Horizons\n\nThis simple summary workflow is just the tip of the iceberg. n8n offers hundreds of integrations and a wide array of AI capabilities.
